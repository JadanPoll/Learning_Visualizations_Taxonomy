# FKEN — Fractal Knowledge & Ecosystem Navigation
**Specification 1.1 · Hierarchically indexed**

## 0. Purpose and governing principles

### FKEN-00 — Objective

**FKEN-00.1 — Purpose.** Provide a persistent, low-friction framework for navigating a large, heterogeneous intellectual and engineering ecosystem.

**FKEN-00.2 — Intended capability.** Enable the investigator to recognize interesting possibilities, orient within unfamiliar domains, identify relevant knowledge and expertise, recognize consequential ignorance, and pursue or coordinate promising investigations without requiring encyclopedic mastery.

**FKEN-00.3 — Optimization objective.** Maximize useful conceptual variance, navigational capability, and accumulated intellectual leverage while minimizing extraneous friction, redundant encounters, and knowledge loss.

**FKEN-00.4 — Directionality.** These are directional objectives, not claims of a globally optimal arrangement. The framework need not know the eventual destination or complete solution to support a justified next action.

### FKEN-01 — Division of responsibility

**FKEN-01.1 — Navigation.** FKEN organizes domains, familiarity, resources, and relationships activated by questions.

**FKEN-01.2 — Scientific investigation.** The Richer Scientific Method (RSM) governs problem representation, decomposition, hypotheses, uncertainty, investigation, evidence, and justified conclusions.

**FKEN-01.3 — Engineering implementation.** The Richer Engineering Method (REM) governs operational necessity, design choices, implementation costs, friction, and architectural parsimony.

**FKEN-01.4 — Operational systems.** Specialized tools acquire, filter, present, annotate, preserve, update, and otherwise operate upon information.

**FKEN-01.5 — Separation.** These responsibilities may interact or share infrastructure. They must not be merged merely for conceptual elegance or completeness.

### FKEN-02 — Economy

**FKEN-02.1 — Necessity.** Introduce a structural or operational component only when its expected benefit justifies its complexity and recurring cost.

**FKEN-02.2 — Friction.** Minimize extraneous navigation, repeated searching, avoidable context reconstruction, unnecessary annotation, and interface operations that interrupt thought.

**FKEN-02.3 — Productive difficulty.** Do not confuse extraneous friction with the intellectual difficulty of genuine problems, unfamiliar ideas, or substantive disagreement.

**FKEN-02.4 — Variance.** Prefer encounters that reveal consequentially different mechanisms, assumptions, constraints, applications, or possibilities over repeated presentations of substantially the same material.

**FKEN-02.5 — Compression.** Collapse redundancy only when consequential differences remain recoverable.

# I. Persistent knowledge geometry

### FKEN-03 — Planar fractal domain tree

**FKEN-03.1 — Structure.** Organize central ideas into a planar, recursively subdividable tree of domains.

**FKEN-03.2 — Domain.** A domain is a useful conceptual territory, not necessarily an academic discipline or conventional subject category.

**FKEN-03.3 — Subdivision.** A domain may contain child domains representing distinctions worth navigating independently. Children may themselves subdivide recursively.

**FKEN-03.4 — Lazy growth.** Create or subdivide domains only when actual encounters or anticipated navigation justify the distinction. Do not require an exhaustive taxonomy.

**FKEN-03.5 — Sparse validity.** A domain may exist without children, substantial resources, or populated tower regions. Sparse nodes are valid.

**FKEN-03.6 — Interpretation.** Parent-child relationships express useful conceptual organization, not necessarily strict logical containment, prerequisite order, or exclusive membership.

### FKEN-04 — Navigational placement

**FKEN-04.1 — Placement criterion.** A resource's primary navigational location should be where the investigator would most naturally and frequently seek it.

**FKEN-04.2 — Question-based evaluation.** When multiple placements are plausible, compare the questions and navigation paths likely to occasion retrieval, using RSM to evaluate the alternatives.

**FKEN-04.3 — Utility over taxonomy.** Prefer expected navigational usefulness over supposedly perfect ontological classification.

**FKEN-04.4 — Revision.** Primary placement is provisional and may change when observed retrieval behavior, new evidence, or new questions justify it.

**FKEN-04.5 — Identity.** Changing placement must not change the underlying resource's identity or require duplicating its canonical record.

### FKEN-05 — Complexity towers

**FKEN-05.1 — Geometry.** Each domain node anchors a triangular complexity tower extending into the third dimension. Its narrow apex represents tacitness; its intermediate region represents scaffolding; its broad base represents gas.

**FKEN-05.2 — Tacitness.** The apex contains essential, readily usable principles, formulas, operations, and intuitions that support basic reasoning and action within the domain.

**FKEN-05.3 — Scaffolding.** The intermediate region represents broader operational knowledge—methods, procedures, models, patterns, and technical details the investigator should recognize, reconstruct, or retrieve when necessary without maintaining continuous mastery.

**FKEN-05.4 — Gas.** The broad base contains high-variance encounters with specialized ideas, implementations, inventions, failures, histories, organizations, people, and other possibilities. Loose recognition or external recoverability may suffice; continuous recall is not required.

**FKEN-05.5 — Geometric meaning.** Increasing tower breadth represents increasing scope of potentially useful familiarity and decreasing requirements for immediate internalization. It does not impose fixed information quantities.

**FKEN-05.6 — Functional distinction.** Tower regions represent different cognitive and navigational functions, not merely three ordered degrees of expertise.

### FKEN-06 — Tower growth and certification

**FKEN-06.1 — Independence.** Each domain has its own potential tower. Its regions may grow independently and remain unequally populated.

**FKEN-06.2 — Role-dependent placement.** The same kind of source can support different tower regions. Resource type does not determine familiarity level.

**FKEN-06.3 — Available knowledge.** Knowledge is *available* when its relevant source or expertise can be located.

**FKEN-06.4 — Familiar knowledge.** Knowledge is *familiar* when the investigator recognizes it or can identify the circumstances in which it may matter.

**FKEN-06.5 — Operational knowledge.** Knowledge is *operational* when the investigator can apply, reconstruct, or appropriately retrieve the relevant method.

**FKEN-06.6 — Tacit knowledge.** Knowledge is *tacit* when its essential operations are sufficiently internalized for ready deployment without substantial reconstruction.

**FKEN-06.7 — Evidence of competence.** Resource availability, encounter frequency, and tower richness do not by themselves establish operational competence or tacitness. Stronger claims require appropriate evidence of use.

**FKEN-06.8 — Personal relativity.** Tower placement describes the investigator's relationship to knowledge, not the intrinsic difficulty or universal importance of a topic.

# II. Resources and local landscapes

### FKEN-07 — Gas elements

**FKEN-07.1 — Definition.** A *gas element* is an identifiable resource or encounter constituting part of the gas region of a domain tower.

**FKEN-07.2 — Examples of eligible elements.** Gas elements may include publications, patents, companies, professors, communities, repositories, newsletters, biographies, projects, and technical observations.

**FKEN-07.3 — Location.** Gas elements are constituents of gas, not a separate layer appended to the tower.

**FKEN-07.4 — Retention expectation.** Encountering a gas element does not obligate complete consumption, annotation, mastery, or persistent conscious recall.

**FKEN-07.5 — Subsequent learning.** Study or use of a gas element may contribute to scaffolding or tacitness. The source and the capability developed from it remain distinguishable.

**FKEN-07.6 — Canonical identity.** An element should retain one identifiable underlying record even when relevant to several domains or investigations.

### FKEN-08 — Landscaplets and domainlets

**FKEN-08.1 — Landscaplet.** A gas element may contain a *landscaplet*: a lazily identified bag of domainlets describing the resource's specific conceptual coverage, activities, or capabilities.

**FKEN-08.2 — Domainlet.** A domainlet is a compact descriptor of relevant conceptual territory. It may reference an existing domain or subdomain without requiring the creation of another node.

**FKEN-08.3 — Selectivity.** A landscaplet need not enumerate all subjects associated with its gas element. Record distinctions that improve recognition, navigation, or retrieval.

**FKEN-08.4 — Cross-domain coverage.** Domainlets may refer to unrelated branches of the planar tree and may vary in specificity and relevance.

**FKEN-08.5 — One identity, multiple views.** A gas element can appear in several domain views through its landscaplet without duplicating its canonical record.

**FKEN-08.6 — Scope distinction.** Primary placement indicates where an element is normally sought; its landscaplet indicates the particular territories it touches.

**FKEN-08.7 — Evidential qualification.** Distinguish demonstrated technical coverage or capability from incidental mentions, inferred relevance, and unverified claims.

**FKEN-08.8 — Structural boundary.** A landscaplet is not inherently a subtree, a separate complexity tower, or a permanent graph of all relationships involving the element.

### FKEN-09 — Resource categories and filtering

**FKEN-09.1 — Independent dimensions.** Distinguish a resource's type, primary domain location, specific conceptual coverage, and role within the tower.

**FKEN-09.2 — Categories.** Courses, communities, companies, professors, publications, tools, and similar resource types may serve as filtering categories.

**FKEN-09.3 — Visual coding.** Resource categories may be color-coded or otherwise visually distinguished to support rapid perception and selection.

**FKEN-09.4 — Composable filtering.** Support filtering by domain, tower region, and resource category independently or in combination.

**FKEN-09.5 — Partition interpretation.** Category partitions within a tower are presentation and navigation mechanisms, not additional domain hierarchies.

**FKEN-09.6 — Nonexclusive roles.** A resource may serve multiple functions; category filters must not imply that all resources of a given type belong exclusively to one tower region.

# III. Question-conditioned landscaping

### FKEN-10 — Dynamic landscapes

**FKEN-10.1 — Purpose.** Construct a *question-conditioned landscape* to identify which domains, resources, mechanisms, expertise, and relationships become relevant to a particular investigation.

**FKEN-10.2 — Inputs.** The landscape depends on the operative question, currently known evidence, relevant assumptions, constraints, and investigative purpose.

**FKEN-10.3 — Selectivity.** Activate only relationships plausibly consequential to the question. The complete ecosystem need not be represented.

**FKEN-10.4 — Generation.** Landscapes may be constructed through human reasoning, AI assistance, or their combination.

**FKEN-10.5 — Epistemic discipline.** Apply RSM to distinguish established relationships, conditional claims, hypotheses, and unresolved uncertainties.

**FKEN-10.6 — Revision.** The activated landscape may change as the question, evidence, constraints, or understanding develops.

### FKEN-11 — Weighted highways

**FKEN-11.1 — Representation.** Visualize an activated landscape as weighted graph highways connecting the broad bases of relevant domain towers.

**FKEN-11.2 — Strength.** Highway thickness represents the relationship's importance or relevance under the operative question.

**FKEN-11.3 — Conditionality.** A highway's activation and weight may differ between questions even when the connected domains remain unchanged.

**FKEN-11.4 — Relationship types.** Highways may express dependencies, shared mechanisms, transferable methods, competing constraints, relevant expertise, or other justified relationships.

**FKEN-11.5 — Strength versus confidence.** Relevance, evidential confidence, and similarity are distinct properties. A thick highway does not necessarily indicate a well-established connection.

**FKEN-11.6 — Geometric convention.** Highways connect tower bases to provide visual space and clarity. Their placement does not restrict the underlying relationship to gas-level knowledge.

**FKEN-11.7 — Persistence boundary.** An activated highway is not automatically a permanent structural edge.

### FKEN-12 — Landscape memory

**FKEN-12.1 — Selective preservation.** Preserve investigation landscapes when their expected future navigational or investigative value justifies the recording cost.

**FKEN-12.2 — Question context.** A preserved record should identify the operative big-picture question and consequential assumptions or constraints.

**FKEN-12.3 — Activations.** Record the domains, resources, and consequential relationships activated by that question.

**FKEN-12.4 — Investigation outcome.** Preserve significant findings, obstructions, uncertainty, and unresolved directions when useful.

**FKEN-12.5 — Provenance.** Maintain sufficient references to distinguish observed evidence from interpretation and speculation.

**FKEN-12.6 — Reuse.** Previous landscapes may inform later investigations but must not be treated as universally valid decompositions.

**FKEN-12.7 — Economy.** Not every question, connection, or tentative association requires permanent storage.

# IV. The landscape of questions

### FKEN-13 — Question records and geometry

**FKEN-13.1 — Definition.** The accumulating collection of preserved investigation questions and their activated landscapes constitutes a *landscape of questions*.

**FKEN-13.2 — Complementarity.** The domain tree organizes conceptual territory and available capabilities; the question landscape records how that territory becomes relevant under particular problems.

**FKEN-13.3 — Incremental development.** The question landscape develops through actual investigations, not exhaustive advance enumeration.

**FKEN-13.4 — Reconsideration.** Questions and their activations may be revised when later investigations reveal overlooked conditions, better decompositions, or stronger connections.

### FKEN-14 — Question-family consolidation

**FKEN-14.1 — Family formation.** Consolidate questions into a more general family when their shared structure is consequential enough to improve future navigation.

**FKEN-14.2 — Bottom-up generalization.** Broader questions may emerge by comparing specific investigations; they need not be imposed before those investigations occur.

**FKEN-14.3 — Preserved distinctions.** Consolidation must retain differences in assumptions, boundary conditions, dependencies, and activated capabilities that affect the investigation.

**FKEN-14.4 — Overlap.** A question may participate in multiple families. The question landscape is not required to be a strict tree.

**FKEN-14.5 — Navigation priors.** Family-level patterns may guide future landscaping but do not mandate an identical decomposition for every member.

**FKEN-14.6 — Lazy consolidation.** Do not create higher-level families merely for taxonomic completeness. Consolidate when doing so reduces future orientation or investigation cost.

### FKEN-15 — Evidence-directed scaffolding

**FKEN-15.1 — Purpose.** Use the accumulated question landscape to identify knowledge and capabilities whose deeper internalization could improve navigation across future problems.

**FKEN-15.2 — Activation patterns.** Repeated activation across meaningfully different question families provides evidence that a capability may be broadly useful.

**FKEN-15.3 — Nonindependence.** Repeated appearances in closely related questions must not be counted as independent evidence of far transfer.

**FKEN-15.4 — Priority factors.** Evaluate prospective mastery using question importance, breadth of transfer, reduction in retrieval or reconstruction cost, learning effort, maintenance effort, and available alternatives.

**FKEN-15.5 — Rare capabilities.** Infrequent activation does not imply low value; some capabilities are crucial to rare but consequential problems.

**FKEN-15.6 — Promotion.** Knowledge may be developed from gas-level recognition into scaffolding or tacitness when its expected value justifies doing so.

**FKEN-15.7 — Externalization.** Knowledge may remain in the Library or accessible human expertise when reliable retrieval or consultation is more economical than internalization.

**FKEN-15.8 — Evidential limit.** Co-activation suggests potential usefulness or transfer; it does not prove that mastering the capability will yield that transfer.

# V. Navigation, operation, and continuity

### FKEN-16 — Navigation modes

**FKEN-16.1 — Exploration.** Navigate domains, towers, and filtered resources to encounter varied material, distinguish interesting possibilities, and skip redundancy or uninteresting branches.

**FKEN-16.2 — Investigation.** Begin with a question, construct its relevant landscape, locate existing capabilities, identify gaps, and navigate toward references, expertise, experiments, or actions.

**FKEN-16.3 — Incomplete foresight.** Neither mode requires a complete destination or final solution. Support successive refinements and directionally justified actions.

**FKEN-16.4 — Interchange.** An exploratory encounter may generate an investigation; an investigation may reveal new domains, gas elements, question families, or opportunities for exploration.

### FKEN-17 — Continuity and provenance

**FKEN-17.1 — Recoverability.** Preserve enough source identity, location, and context to recover consequential encounters and previous investigations.

**FKEN-17.2 — Epistemic separation.** Distinguish original evidence, extracted observations, interpretations, speculative connections, and demonstrated capabilities.

**FKEN-17.3 — External Library.** FKEN may index or reference documents and notes contained in a separate Library. Its navigational representation does not replace the underlying archive.

**FKEN-17.4 — Resumption.** Significant unfinished investigations should be recoverable without reconstructing all prior context.

**FKEN-17.5 — Revision.** Correct, relocate, merge, or reclassify navigational records as understanding changes, preserving important provenance.

**FKEN-17.6 — Selective capture.** Do not require detailed annotation of every encounter. The cost of preservation must remain subordinate to the value of actual intellectual activity.

### FKEN-18 — Operational boundary

**FKEN-18.1 — Scope limitation.** FKEN specifies navigation and organization, not the implementation of every operation performed upon its resources.

**FKEN-18.2 — Independent operations.** Newsletter ingestion, community filtering, PDF annotation, problem-book generation, source maintenance, and similar activities remain separate engineering tasks.

**FKEN-18.3 — Tool integration.** Operational tools may share references, metadata, or navigation interfaces without sharing their complete implementations.

**FKEN-18.4 — No monolith requirement.** A centralized navigational view does not require a single application to perform all underlying operations.

**FKEN-18.5 — REM applicability.** Evaluate operational mechanisms according to demonstrated necessity, efficiency, maintenance burden, failure modes, and friction.

### FKEN-19 — Architectural discipline

**FKEN-19.1 — Problem first.** Before introducing a component, identify the specific navigational or operational failure it is intended to address.

**FKEN-19.2 — Alternative mechanisms.** Compare the proposed component with simpler alternatives and existing capabilities.

**FKEN-19.3 — Justification.** Retain components whose benefits justify their implementation, maintenance, and interaction costs.

**FKEN-19.4 — Lazy computation.** Prefer computing question-specific relationships when needed over permanently materializing every possible connection.

**FKEN-19.5 — Epistemic caution.** Do not mistake resource density for understanding, apparent novelty for meaningful variance, or visual prominence for evidential strength.

**FKEN-19.6 — Empirical revision.** Where competing designs remain plausible, test them using actual navigation and investigation tasks rather than resolving the uncertainty by architectural preference.

# Appendix A — Exemplar applications

The following examples are interpretive tests, not additional requirements. They clarify how the clauses interact in realistic situations.

## A.1 — Lazy domain growth

**Situation.** While investigating mechanical systems, the investigator repeatedly encounters mechatronics questions.

**Application.** Create a Mechatronics node beneath Mechanical Engineering if separate navigation becomes useful. The node may initially contain little beyond a few references and gas elements.

Later, particular subjects—actuation, sensors, feedback control—may justify child domains or richer tower content. No complete mechatronics curriculum is required.

**Governing clauses:** FKEN-03.2–03.6; FKEN-05.1–05.6; FKEN-06.1.

**Invalid inference:** Because Mechatronics exists as a node, all its major subjects must be enumerated or its tower completed.

## A.2 — Electronics Discord and its landscaplet

**Situation.** An electronics community contains discussions of soldering, oscilloscopes, RF engineering, PCB design, and embedded debugging.

**Application.** Store the community as a gas element primarily under Electronics. Its landscaplet may contain the domainlets:

`{soldering, oscilloscopes, RF, PCB design, embedded debugging}`

If the investigator later searches for RF measurement expertise, the community may surface through its RF and oscilloscope domainlets.

**Governing clauses:** FKEN-04.1–04.5; FKEN-07.1–07.6; FKEN-08.1–08.8.

**Invalid inference:** The community must be duplicated under every mentioned field, or every domainlet must become a fully populated subdomain.

## A.3 — Company with narrow cross-domain relevance

**Situation.** A robotics company primarily develops locomotion systems but has specific work relevant to battery management.

**Application.** Its primary location remains Robotics if that best serves typical retrieval. Its landscaplet records the particular battery-management coverage.

During an electrical-engineering investigation, the relevant aspect can be surfaced without implying that the company broadly specializes in electrical engineering.

**Governing clauses:** FKEN-04.1–04.5; FKEN-08.4–08.7; FKEN-09.1.

**Invalid inference:** Cross-domain relevance means the complete company record belongs equally to every related domain.

## A.4 — Category filtering within a domain

**Situation.** The investigator wants substantive engineering communities concerning control theory.

**Application.** Navigate to Control Engineering and activate the Social resource filter. Display relevant communities while retaining their canonical records and landscaplets.

Switching to Publications should expose relevant books, papers, or newsletters without changing the domain structure.

**Governing clauses:** FKEN-09.1–09.6; FKEN-16.1.

**Invalid inference:** Communities form a separate conceptual domain or inherently belong to scaffolding rather than gas.

## A.5 — Question-conditioned aircraft landscape

**Situation.** The investigator asks, "How might I build a small RC aircraft?"

**Application.** Establish the relevant requirements and constraints. Activate appropriate domains, potentially including aerodynamics, propulsion, structures, control, electronics, fabrication, and verification.

Weighted highways reflect consequential relationships under those requirements. For example, propulsion, battery mass, and flight performance may form strong connections.

A commercial-aircraft question would yield a different landscape despite overlapping domain labels.

**Governing clauses:** FKEN-10.1–10.6; FKEN-11.1–11.7.

**Invalid inference:** Every aircraft investigation shares a fixed decomposition, or highway thickness measures certainty.

## A.6 — Remembering an investigation

**Situation.** The RC-aircraft investigation reveals an important relationship between motor selection, battery mass, and aircraft performance.

**Application.** Preserve the operative question, its relevant constraints, activated domains, meaningful dependencies, and source references.

Do not necessarily retain every candidate relation or every discarded conversational branch.

**Governing clauses:** FKEN-12.1–12.7; FKEN-17.1–17.6.

**Invalid inference:** Every activated highway deserves permanent storage or is valid for all future aircraft questions.

## A.7 — Emergence of question families

**Situation.** Over time, the investigator asks:

- How do I build an RC car?
- How do I build an RC aircraft?
- How do I build a full-size car?
- How do I build a computer?

**Application.** Compare the investigations and their activated landscapes.

RC-car and RC-aircraft questions may justify a family concerning remotely controlled systems. Broader recurring structures may justify a family concerning technical system construction.

These generalizations emerge from specific investigations rather than requiring a predefined taxonomy.

**Governing clauses:** FKEN-13.1–13.4; FKEN-14.1–14.6.

**Invalid inference:** All construction questions possess identical requirements, or a question must have only one parent family.

## A.8 — Discovering valuable scaffolding

**Situation.** Interface design, circuit analysis, and experimental debugging recur across several distinct investigations.

**Application.** Examine whether deeper internalization of these capabilities would substantially reduce future navigation or investigation costs.

Compare their reuse across genuinely different question families, learning costs, and alternatives such as reliable references or specialist consultation.

A rarely used verification technique may still deserve mastery if it is indispensable in consequential circumstances.

**Governing clauses:** FKEN-06.3–06.8; FKEN-15.1–15.8.

**Invalid inference:** Frequency alone determines what should become tacit, or recurring co-activation proves far transfer.

## A.9 — Efficient newsletter navigation

**Situation.** The investigator wants to stay informed about semiconductor manufacturing without repeatedly encountering redundant commentary.

**Application.** FKEN locates the relevant domain and its newsletters, companies, experts, and other resources.

An operational system handles newsletter acquisition, filtering, deduplication, and presentation. Significant discoveries can enrich gas elements or produce new domainlets and question records.

**Governing clauses:** FKEN-01.1–01.5; FKEN-02.1–02.5; FKEN-18.1–18.5.

**Invalid inference:** FKEN must itself implement a newsletter reader or that adding an elaborate ranking engine is necessarily justified.

## A.10 — Navigating without a predetermined end state

**Situation.** A discussion with an engineer suggests an unfamiliar but intriguing technical possibility.

**Application.** Use existing familiarity to develop a provisional representation and identify missing knowledge. Locate references or expertise, pursue a small justified investigation, and revise the operative question as evidence accumulates.

If the investigation reveals a useful new domain distinction, gas element, or recurring question structure, preserve it selectively.

**Governing clauses:** FKEN-00.2–00.4; FKEN-10.2–10.6; FKEN-16.2–16.4; FKEN-17.4.

**Invalid inference:** The investigator must first possess a complete domain map, commit to an end goal, or fully master the relevant subject before acting.

# Appendix B — Application certificates

An AI or human applying FKEN should identify the **specific subclauses actually used**, the evidence supporting the decision, and any consequential uncertainty.

References to FKEN establish which rule applies; they do not themselves establish that the rule's factual conditions are satisfied.

### B.1 — Minimal certificate

**Question:** What decision or navigation problem is being addressed?

**Applicable clauses:** Cite only the relevant `FKEN-XX.Y` identifiers.

**Evidence:** Identify observations, sources, or previously established facts supporting the application.

**Interpretation:** Explain how the evidence satisfies the referenced clauses.

**Decision or next action:** State the resulting placement, retrieval, investigation, or revision.

**Uncertainty:** State material assumptions, missing evidence, or competing interpretations.

**Example:**

**Question:** Where should an electronics community with active RF discussions be accessible?

**Applicable clauses:** FKEN-04.1, FKEN-08.4, FKEN-08.5, FKEN-08.7.

**Evidence:** The community is primarily organized around electronics, with a recurring RF-measurement discussion channel.

**Interpretation:** Electronics is a plausible primary retrieval location; RF is specific cross-domain coverage.

**Decision:** Retain one community record under Electronics and expose an RF-measurement domainlet through its landscaplet.

**Uncertainty:** The depth of expertise represented by the RF channel remains unverified.

### B.2 — Citation discipline

**B.2.1.** Cite the narrowest applicable subclauses rather than entire sections when possible.

**B.2.2.** Distinguish normative claims from empirical observations and speculative interpretations.

**B.2.3.** Do not cite clauses merely to make an ordinary decision appear formally justified.

**B.2.4.** When clauses conflict in application, identify the tension and use RSM to compare alternatives. Do not silently choose one.

**B.2.5.** When the specification does not govern a decision, say so. Do not invent a requirement by extrapolating from an example.

### B.3 — Identifier stability

**B.3.1.** A clause identifier refers to a specific normative proposition, not merely a paragraph location.

**B.3.2.** Adding a proposition should not silently change the meanings of existing identifiers.

**B.3.3.** Materially revised propositions should carry an explicit version change or supersession record.

**B.3.4.** Examples are interpretive evidence of intended application, not independently binding rules.

# Specification boundary and summary

FKEN does not guarantee complete knowledge coverage, optimal learning, correct interdisciplinary inference, or automatic mastery.

It provides a persistent, lazily grown domain geometry; per-domain towers distinguishing tacitness, scaffolding, and gas; gas elements containing local landscaplets; question-conditioned highways for interdisciplinary navigation; and an accumulated question landscape for recovering investigations and identifying valuable scaffolding.

Its success should be evaluated by whether it materially improves the investigator's ability to **recognize, navigate, retrieve, relate, and mobilize knowledge and expertise** while preserving accumulated intellectual leverage.

All additional machinery remains subject to RSM's evidential discipline and REM's necessity, economy, and implementation criteria.
